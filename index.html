More

Long-press the map (or right-click on the web) to open a spot with its trail mile, Google Maps, Street View and a Find-nearby row. A plain tap just reads the map. This switch only controls whether that dialog can also move your location.

Average riding speed

An overall average, wheels-out to wheels-in, with the stops already inside it — not the moving average a bike computer shows. Lunch, locks, photographs and a puncture all come out of it, so it sits well below your road speed: 10 to 13 is honest for a loaded touring bike, and 12 on the road is more like 9 all-in. It decides where you’d be at each forecast hour, so the weather layer and the Outlook both hang off it — and it is also on the Ride plan tab, which is where it matters most. Miles a day is only the target Auto-plan aims at; once a plan exists, each day’s distance is that day’s own. Speed is currently 12 mph.

Your own layers

Adding one moved to the map, under Add your own at the foot of the layers card — the same card that lists it afterwards, where the × beside its name takes it off again. Whatever you add is switched on and drawn at the top of the list, and can be dragged into whatever order you want. An ArcGIS URL is a single layer’s endpoint — the one ending in a number. It’s fetched over New York, up to 2,000 features, and stored as the URL so it stays current. A GPX is stored as its points, since there’s nowhere to fetch it back from. After a reload they’re listed but off, so nothing you added last week spends a fetch before you ask for it.

Route and every trail-mile are measured along Judd EST 2026 — a ridden plan, not the state's published centreline. It follows the official route for most of its 582 miles, then takes its own way through Putnam County and, past Lockport, leaves the canal for Lake Ontario and comes down the Niagara into Buffalo. Facility pins load live from NY State's public service; confirm a specific business before relying on it.
